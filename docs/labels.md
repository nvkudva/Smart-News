## What the labels say

### The two labellers agree about as often as a weak rater pair

| | |
|---|---|
| exact agreement | **22 / 40 (55%)** |
| within one step on noise–marginal–worth | 33 / 40 (82%) |
| opposite verdicts (worth vs noise) | **7 / 40 (18%)** |
| Cohen's κ | **0.32** |

κ of 0.32 is fair-to-weak. Chance agreement alone is 34% here, because both
labellers use the three categories unevenly — Vijay called 18 of 40 worth,
Claude 12 — so the 55% raw figure flatters it.

**This is the finding that matters most, and it is not about the gate.** Seven
stories out of forty got flatly opposite verdicts, and they are not edge cases
the labelling instructions could have caught:

| # | Vijay / Claude | story |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | worth / noise | Karnataka CM and colleagues to ride an open-top double-decker to a cabinet meeting |
| 9 | worth / noise | Inc42 roundup — Clean Label Brands vs FSSAI, PhonePe eyes 2027 IPO |
| 11 | worth / noise | Brad Gerstner says AI 'negativity' is a political agenda, plus two stock picks |
| 13 | noise / worth | A Bihar banyan is at least 700 years old, and science has proved it |
| 16 | worth / noise | Mysterious trader moves $122m ahead of the Fed's 2pm decision |
| 21 | worth / noise | Sonia Rovai appointed Disney Italy VP of Original Production |
| 24 | worth / noise | HT Morning Brief for 17 Sept |

Rows 9 and 24 are *aggregator formats* — a roundup and a morning brief — which
one labeller reads as several stories' worth of signal and the other as a chore
of exactly the kind `routineShapes` exists to refuse. Row 21 is a trade
appointment: real news to the Entertainment desk, filler everywhere else.

So "quality" as used in the cost analysis is not yet one thing. A gate cannot
be tuned against a target two careful labellers disagree about 18% of the time;
what the disagreements have in common is a *category* question — is a roundup a
story, is a trade appointment a story — which is answerable in the abstract,
before any model or threshold is chosen.

### Front-page prominence does not separate worth from noise

Bands are how many title-tier front pages matched the cluster's entities: 0,
1, or 2+.

| band | n | Vijay says worth | Claude says worth | both say noise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 14 | 5 (36%) | 4 (29%) | 3 (21%) |
| 1 | 13 | 6 (46%) | 4 (31%) | 3 (23%) |
| 2 | 13 | 7 (54%) | 4 (31%) | **4 (31%)** |

Vijay's labels trend the right way — 36% to 54% across the bands — and the
trend is not significant at this size (Fisher exact, band 0 vs band 2,
**p = 0.45**). Claude's labels are flat: 29%, 31%, 31%, **p = 1.0**.

The column that should settle it is the last one. If prominence were a quality
gate, the stories both labellers call noise would thin out as the band rises.
They do the opposite — 21%, 23%, 31%. Band 2 holds the NGT query about idol
immersion preparedness (#12), the Dean Jones–Chennai connection piece (#18),
and Trump ad-libbing at a rally (#27), while band 0 holds the EPFO wage-ceiling
rise (#14) and the Supreme Court reassessment ruling (#15) — two of the most
clearly consequential stories in the sample.

That is what an entity match measures rather than what it was hoped to
measure: whether a cluster mentions names the front pages are full of today.
Trump, a Test cricketer and a state government are on every front page, so
anything naming them scores. A tax ruling that changes what a million people
owe names nobody famous.

### Consequence for the cost analysis

[docs/summarisation-cost.md](summarisation-cost.md) §4a proposes widening
title-tier to 25–30 front pages so prominence becomes dense enough to gate
`worthWriting`. **This sample is evidence against the gating half of that, and
it does not touch the widening half.**

The widening is still worth doing on its own terms: more front pages cost no
neurons, and prominence remains a defensible *ranking* signal — what editors
led with today — even where it fails as a quality filter. What this sample
refuses is the step after it, of admitting or rejecting single-source clusters
on band alone. At 33% of the queue admitted, band 2 buys a worth-rate of 54%
against 36% at band 0 on the more generous labeller and nothing at all on the
other, which is not a gate.

### What would settle it

1. **Fix the target before the signal.** Decide whether a roundup, a morning
   brief and a trade appointment are stories. Three rules would remove three of
   the seven opposite verdicts, and they are rules `routineShapes` can apply
   without a model.
2. **Re-label with that definition**, and only then measure agreement again. A
   κ below about 0.6 means the labels cannot adjudicate any gate.
3. **Then test prominence at size.** Forty rows across three bands cannot
   detect anything smaller than a very large effect; the p-values above are the
   honest statement of that, not a rejection.

Until then the cap in `summarisePending` is doing the work, and it is doing it
without needing to know which stories are good — which is the argument for
having shipped the cap first.
